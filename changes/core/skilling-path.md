# LEVELING PATH AND EVERY SKILL

For players:
- A leveling path: the zones to quest in from your level to 60, in order, with the level each should leave you at. It shows under the guide window (also in Glance), at the top of the main window and with `/wt path`, and *Follow path* chains the zone guides so each hands over to the next by itself, after you have turned in what is finished and finished what is nearly done (85% by default). It has its own setting and does not need the experimental XP model, which stays off.
- Professions: crafting paths pick the cheapest way to your skill goal and reach 300 for all six crafting professions, First Aid and Cooking; Mining, Herbalism, Skinning and Fishing get advice on what gives skill and where.
- A button for your Hearthstone or teleport when the guide asks for it. In a party the addon offers to share your quests for the zone or dungeon you are in: it asks once per place, and shares only if you say yes (`/wt share` does it on demand; Party settings can make it automatic or turn it off).

Please check:
- Type `/wt path`, switch on Follow path and play an hour: do the zones and levels make sense, and is questing smooth? Then type `/wt perf` and send the lines: they name the slowest calls.
- Click the Hearthstone or teleport button when the guide shows one, and group up in a zone or dungeon with shareable quests: does each work, and without an "action blocked" message?
- Open a crafting path and compare its total with the shopping list after an auction scan; open First Aid, Cooking, Fishing and a gathering profession and check your skill is read correctly.

Developer notes:
- New `LevelingPath.lua` (see `LEVELING_PATH.md`): `ns.LevelingPath(callback)` simulates zone by zone from the character's level over `ns.LevelingZoneInventory()` (new, in `LevelingGuides.lua`), cooperatively, cached on a signature of level, a quarter of the XP bar, quest log, history count, followed guide and kill share. It records total experience on `PLAYER_XP_UPDATE` and quest reward experience on `QUEST_TURNED_IN` in `ns.char.xpPace`; `ns.LevelingXPMultiplier()` blends an assumed 1.8 with the measured ratio over thirty quests. `ns.LevelingPathNextGuide` feeds `zoneProjection` in `GuideXP.lua`; `ns.LevelingPathRecommendation` is registered as the `path` recommendation provider and the `leveling` provider tries the path's first zone first. `/wt path`, a path window, a probe line. On screen: `ns.CreateLevelingPathStrip`/`ns.UpdateLevelingPathStrip` add a one-line strip to the stack under the guide window (`Navigation.lua`, `AreaObjectives.lua`; option `levelingPathStrip`), and `RecommendedUI.lua` renders a four-row "Your leveling path" block with the *Follow path* button at the top of the main page in both layouts: above the recommended-zone card on the classic page and above "Next for you" on the new Hub's Play page. The `path:` recommendation item is left out of the rows and the "More" grid while the block is shown. The path starts on the continent the character stands on, and a zone the character stands in comes first when it has four quests on offer or any of its quests already in the log; each quest in the log adds 25% to its zone's score for the first step (up to four). A followed guide is matched to its path zone by `mapID`, `homeMapID` or zone name. Path mode (`ns.FollowLevelingPath`, `ns.StopLevelingPathMode`, `ns.char.levelingPathMode`, `/wt path follow|stop`): `ns.LevelingPathChain(state)`, called from the strip update, starts the next zone's guide with `ns.StartPartyRoute` three seconds after a `zone-exit` stop with a `nextGuide` or a completed route, outside combat and planning, at most once per zone per two minutes and three times per ten minutes. Finish first: `ns.QuestProgressFraction`/`ns.QuestNearlyDone` (new, `GuidePolicy.lua`; option `finishNearlyDone` = "85", "70" or "off") make `levelingValue` return true for an in-log quest at or above the threshold, so the route keeps it and the zone is not reported finished; `ns.ZoneUnfinishedBusiness(guide)` lists finished and nearly finished quests of the zone in the log and the chain holds the hand-over for them with one chat line. `ns.NextLevelingGuide` (path first, baseline second) now also feeds the zone-finished notice in `GuideOptimizer.lua`.
- Path cost (fixed after an in-game profile showed 271 frames over 50 ms in three minutes): the background calculation yielded by item count, in slices that were 4 ms in the host and tens of milliseconds in the client, about seventy in a row, on every quest-log change. It now yields on a 1.5 ms per-frame budget read from `debugprofilestop`, reads completion once per calculation, works out twelve zones routinely (`ns.LevelingPathWhole` for all of them), rebuilds an existing path at most every 30 s unless a caller is waiting, and only schedules a refresh when the first two zones or the level target changed. The strip is a borderless line (no fill in Glance) without the changing XP-per-hour figure. The strip and travel button are re-anchored only when their anchor changes, and the hand-over hold is evaluated once a second.
- Planner cost (after an in-game profile showed the addon at 70% of the game's time for 43 seconds, 7 FPS, while `/wt perf` saw 0.3%: the work was in the route planner's timer callbacks, which were not timed): the cooperative planner yielded every N items (`work % 150`, `% 200`, `% 1000`...). Twelve such sites in `FixedGuides.lua`, `FixedRouteOptimizer.lua`, `FixedTravelCost.lua`, `GuideOptimizer.lua`, `QuestFlow.lua`, `GuideXP.lua` and one in `WorldQuestLocations.lua` now ask `ns.PlanShouldYield(work, N)`: with a clock it yields once 5 ms of the slice are used (`ns.planSliceStart`, set by the driver in `GuideOptimizer.lua`), without one it counts as before. The route produced is unchanged; planning takes more frames and each is short. The driver is timed as `plan`, and the per-frame scripts of `MinimapRoute.lua`, `StandaloneArrow.lua` and `TravelTrails.lua` as `minimapline`, `arrow` and `trails`, so `/wt perf` covers them. Not addressed: the single callback that finishes a plan (`ShowGuideOnMap` and two refreshes, about 200 ms in the host) is still one long frame.
- Recorder coverage and the closed window (after `/wt perf` showed 0.6% in a window where the game ran at 9 FPS, and `refresh:window` at about 60 ms per refresh): all sixty `C_Timer.After` call sites now go through `ns.After(label, delay, callback)` (`Perf.lua`), which times the callback as `plan`, `path` or `timer:<file>`; a test fails if a file starts a timer any other way. `ns.Refresh` no longer renders the main window while it is closed when the refresh was caused by a game event (`ns.handlingGameEvent`, set by the dispatcher); interface-initiated refreshes render as before. New `/wt glance` toggles Glance.
- New `HudDiagnostics.lua`: `/wt hud` prints the guide window's shown/alpha/scale/size/position/strata, its fill and border colours, the state of the title, step line, pause, menu and close controls and of the attached strip, travel button and quest-item button, and any error raised by the window's add-on parts; `/wt hud reset` restores the full, solid window at its default place and size. The probe includes the report. `ns.GuardHud` runs the travel button and path strip (update and creation) under `pcall` so a failure in either cannot stop `ns.UpdateNavigation`; the message is kept in `ns.hudErrors`. `ns.UpdateNavigationBackground` now colours each panel by name, since a list literal with a missing panel ended at the gap.
- Guide window and combat (from `/wt hud` in play: after `/wt glance` during a fight the window and every child reported no position while still "shown"; before it all had coordinates): the window has secure children (the quest-item button, now the travel button), so it may not be resized or laid out again in combat. `ns.GlanceActive()` is the mode as applied; it follows `guideGlance` only outside combat (`ns.GlancePending()` while a switch waits), and the three reads in `Navigation.lua` and the strip use it. `ns.LayoutNavigation` no longer calls `SetHeight` in combat (`frame.heightPending`). On `PLAYER_REGEN_ENABLED` a waiting switch or height is applied and, if `ns.NavigationPlaced()` is false, `ns.ReanchorNavigation` fastens the window to its saved or default place and notes it in `ns.hudNotes`. The quest-item button is re-anchored only when its anchor changes and never in combat. `/wt hud` now prints the window's anchors, whether the game can place it, combat, the applied mode and any repair. A later report showed the same loss of position out of combat (one anchor to UIParent, size 360x150, `GetLeft` nil), so combat is not the whole cause; what every case has in common is a change of the window's height. Therefore: Glance no longer changes the height (it kept 150 against the full window's 168 or more); after any `SetHeight` the placement is checked and `ns.ReanchorNavigation` (size and anchor stated again, as a reset does) runs if it was lost; and `ns.HealNavigation`, called on every update, does the same for a shown window with no position, at most every two seconds, outside combat, ten times a session. `/wt hud` also prints `IsRectValid`, `IsUserPlaced`, `IsProtected`, `IsAnchoringRestricted` and the position of a plain control frame given the same anchor and size. The engine-side reason is not known.
- New `QuestCleanup.lua`: `ns.UselessQuests()` lists in-log quests whose reward at the character's level is under 30% of a typical quest's, for which `ns.LevelingValue` is false, that are not ready, not nearly done (`ns.QuestNearlyDone`), not dungeon, class, profession or repeatable quests, not kept by the player (`ns.LowValueQuestKept`, new accessor in `GuidePolicy.lua`) and known to the catalogue. Option `questCleanup` (`ask` default, `auto`, `off`): six seconds after `PLAYER_LEVEL_UP`, and on `/wt clean`, `ask` shows the list with Abandon them / Keep them / Always clean; `auto` abandons and prints what went. `ns.AbandonQuests` uses `SetSelectedQuest`, `SetAbandonQuest` and `AbandonQuest`, one quest per 0.4 s, never in combat, restoring the selection and refreshing once at the end. TOC: `QuestCleanup.lua` after `QuestShare.lua`.

TOC: `Perf.lua` after `Core.lua`.
- XP model off again, path decoupled (after play showed the guide with no steps: "Finish a prerequisite first: Infiltration ... Later"): with the model on, `levelingValue` returned `in-log-low-value` for quest 533 in the log ("worth ~15800 XP for a ~28 min trip") and the Hillsbrad 31-40 guide planned zero stops; with it off the same guide plans the Alterac objective and the turn-in. A test reproduces both. `xpModel` defaults to false again and installs an earlier build moved to true are moved back once (`xpModelOffAgain`, replacing `xpModelDefault`); its setting is labelled experimental. Without the model there is no `zone-exit` stop, so `ns.LevelingPathChain` also hands over when the followed zone was the path's first zone and no longer is (nothing left worth the character's level), for guides the path itself had first (`onPath`); a zone the player chose against the path is not left on the path's say-so. The host fixture pins `levelingPath` off unless a test asks for it.
- Stuck at the end of a zone (seen in play: "Remaining guide steps are skipped ... Later" while the path still named that zone and nothing handed over): `ns.LevelingPathChain` now also treats a guide left with a notice and no step (`stop.kind == "notice"`, no quest ID, `guide.pendingReason` set, not planning) as finished, re-reading the window's state after the three-second delay so a passing notice does not count. On a hand-over the zone is recorded in `ns.char.levelingPathSetAside[zone] = level` and the simulation does not offer it again until the character is past that level, because the path counts quests a guide cannot route. A quest with any skipped step (`ns.GuideQuestHasSkips`, new in `GuideControls.lua`) no longer counts for its zone, and skips are part of the path's signature (`ns.GuideSkipSignature`). Solo elite quests: `levelingValue` returns false for `ns.IsGroupQuest` quests when the player is not grouped, unless `soloEliteQuests` is on or the quest is nearly done.
- Too hard (seen in play: a level 33 character sent into Alterac for the level 36 quest Gol'dir, in the log and "required for Blackmoore's Legacy later in this guide"): new `ns.QuestTooHard(id, level)` in `GuidePolicy.lua` is true for a quest more than `questLevelsAbove` levels (option: "2" default, "3", "any") above the character unless its objectives are complete or nearly so. `levelingValue` returns false for it before any in-log or prerequisite exception; `ns.LevelingWorkAllowed` and the `readyTask` rule in `GuideOptimizer.lua` no longer retain it as a ready hand-in (a quest with no objectives is "ready" on accepting, and its end can be the dangerous part). The existing `level + 3` band check is unchanged. This departs from the comment "Difficulty informs the player's choice; solo status must not hide work", as does the solo elite rule: both follow what the player asked for and both have a setting.

- New `ProfessionPath.lua`: `ns.ProfessionPathPlan(id, start, target, cooperative)` returns the same shape as the old preview (`steps` with `recipe`, `start`, `finish`, `crafts`; `missing`, `start`, `target`) plus `gold`, `fees`, `seconds`, `priced`, `mode`, `widened` and per-step `safe`, `gold`, `acquire`, `fee`, `prepared` (id to `{name, amount}`), `source`. `ns.PlanProfessionPreview` delegates to it; the earlier per-point code remains as a fallback when the file is absent.
- Cost per skill point: reagents at the cheapest of auction/vendor quote or crafting (depth 2; an unpriced intermediate is estimated from its parts, never by a stand-in), minus the vendor sell price of the output, plus crafting seconds times the preference weight (0, 100 or 1000 copper), divided by the recipe's chance plus the expected skill gain of its intermediate crafts. A recipe's training fee and a short errand are charged once per stretch of its use; the dynamic programme carries a per-recipe "continued" cost so the fee is weighed against the saving.
- Smelting: `index()` adds ten pseudo-recipes (`external = {profession = 186, skill = n}`, grey 0) to every profession's `producers`. `allowed` admits one when `ns.gatheringData[186].skill` reaches its requirement, so the path, `ns.ProfessionMaterials` and the guide's preparation steps all pick it up; `craftable` counts it from bags and the step reads "Smelt Copper: 12 at a forge" instead of asking to learn it from the crafting trainer.
- `allowed`, `candidates` and `ns.ProfessionSkillChance` take an optional `widen` flag admitting `source == "vendor"` recipes; defaults are unchanged for every existing caller. The path uses it throughout when `professionVendorRecipes` is on, and otherwise only when the trainer-only path stops short (Tailoring at 255, Blacksmithing at 285, Engineering at 290, First Aid at 210).
